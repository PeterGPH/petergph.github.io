Hi all, and thank you for visiting. My name is Pinhao Gu, and I am a fifth-year Ph.D. student in physical chemistry at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, working with [Prof. Aleksei Aksimentiev](https://bionano.physics.illinois.edu/). Before joining the Aksimentiev group I worked with [Prof. So Hirata](https://hirata-lab.chemistry.illinois.edu/) on finite-temperature electronic structure theory. This site is where I share my research and the occasional personal project.

Research Interests
======
I build forward models that connect molecular structure to measurable signal, and I make those models fast enough to use at scale.

Most of my current work is in **nanopore sensing**. I develop [PETK](https://github.com/PeterGPH/PETK-Pore_Explorer_Toolkit), a finite-element toolkit that predicts ionic current and conductance blockade for solid-state and biological nanopores, and I use it to ask what a nanopore can and cannot reveal about a protein's shape. Alongside the physics, I train **deep-learning surrogates** (Ion-Pot and Ion-Curr) that approximate the same observables at a fraction of the cost, and I work on **agentic AI for computational chemistry** through ChatVMD, an assistant that turns natural-language requests into validated molecular-visualization and analysis actions.

Earlier in my Ph.D. I worked on **finite-temperature many-body theory**, comparing thermal mean-field methods against exact benchmarks. That work appeared in *The Journal of Chemical Physics*.

Broadly, I am interested in scientific machine learning, molecular simulation, quantum chemistry, and GPU/HPC scientific computing — and in the places where a physical model and a learned one can be made to check each other.
